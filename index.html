Before you start

  • Wet the two electrode areas on the strap and wear it snug. The H10 only wakes up on skin contact.
  • Close other apps that are streaming from the strap (Polar Flow syncing in the background is fine).
  • ECG streaming needs H10 firmware 3.0.35 or later. Update it in Polar Flow if the connection works but no trace appears.
  • On iPhone, the download buttons open the share sheet. Choose Save to Files (or AirDrop or Mail) to keep the CSV.
  • Keep this tab in front while recording. The page asks the screen to stay on. Data lives in memory until you download it (roughly 25 MB of CSV per hour).
  • If the strap drops out, the page reconnects by itself and keeps recording. Any gap shows up in the timestamps.
  • Opened as a local file and Connect says Bluetooth isn’t allowed? In Terminal, in the file’s folder, run python3 -m http.server 8000 and open http://localhost:8000/.

Using the charts

  • The ECG is drawn on standard chart paper: 1 mm small squares, 5 mm large squares, 10 mm per millivolt. The square pulse at the left edge is the 1 mV calibration mark.
  • The slider under each chart sets the zoom: left zooms in, right zooms out. The magnifier buttons jump between standard steps. On the ECG, zoom is the paper speed (3.125 to 100 mm/s; 25 mm/s is the clinical standard). On the R–R and heart-rate charts it is the time window (15 seconds to 1 hour).
  • Drag a chart sideways to scroll back in time. Drag back to the newest data, double-tap the chart, or switch Follow on to return to live. The ECG view keeps the last 10 minutes; recordings keep everything.
  • Smoothing applies an exponential moving average to the displayed trace only.

What’s in the files

polar-h10-ecg-….csv
One row per sample (130 per second): elapsed_s from the first sample on the sensor clock, unix_time_ms wall-clock time anchored to this device, sensor_timestamp_ns from the H10, ecg_uV raw value in microvolts (divide by 1000 for mV), and marker (M1, M2 … where you pressed Add marker).
polar-h10-hr-rr-….csv
unix_time_ms, hr_bpm and rr_ms as reported by the strap, one row per R–R interval. Works with HRV tools such as Kubios.
The H10 is a consumer sensor, not a medical device. Nothing is uploaded: the recording stays in this browser until you download it.
